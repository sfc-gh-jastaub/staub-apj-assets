The engagement. Brindabella Group has appointed your firm to rebuild how it determines GST. Brindabella runs three general ledgers, inherited from a decade of acquisitions, and their tax team assembles the group position in Excel every month without ever being able to prove it. You are the tax transformation team on that engagement.

What you are up against. A partner will shortly ask what Brindabella's group GST position is. Before you can even attempt that, you need to see what the three systems actually send — because they do not describe the business the same way.

Before your first block: how to run one that has numbered statements

Some blocks in this lab contain more than one statement, numbered -- 1., -- 2. and so on in the comments. A worksheet renders only the result of the last statement it ran, so running such a block in one go hides the results the step is asking you to look at. Run those statements one at a time: put the cursor inside a statement and press Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter on Windows), or select just that statement before you run. Blocks with a single statement, and blocks where only the final result matters, are unaffected.

Look at the three shapes side by side

Nine rows, three source systems, everything cast to VARCHAR so nothing is silently coerced on the way to your screen. You see exactly what landed.

sql
-- Three source systems, nine rows, everything cast to VARCHAR so nothing is
-- silently coerced. Read down DATE_AS_LANDED, then across the two amount columns.
-- Each subquery is ordered so the sample is the same rows every time you run it.
SELECT * FROM (
    SELECT 'SAP'    AS SOURCE_SYSTEM,
           BELNR::VARCHAR              AS DOC_REF,
           BUKRS::VARCHAR              AS ENTITY_AS_LANDED,
           BUDAT::VARCHAR              AS DATE_AS_LANDED,
           DMBTR::VARCHAR              AS AMOUNT_1,
           NULL::VARCHAR               AS AMOUNT_2,
           MWSTS::VARCHAR              AS TAX_AS_BOOKED,
           MWSKZ::VARCHAR              AS TAX_CODE
    FROM RAW_DATA.ERP_SAP_GL ORDER BY BELNR LIMIT 3)
UNION ALL
SELECT * FROM (
    SELECT 'ORACLE',
           JE_HEADER_ID::VARCHAR,
           LEDGER_ID::VARCHAR,
           GL_DATE::VARCHAR,
           ENTERED_DR::VARCHAR,
           ENTERED_CR::VARCHAR,
           TAX_AMOUNT::VARCHAR,
           TAX_CLASSIFICATION::VARCHAR
    FROM RAW_DATA.ERP_ORACLE_GL ORDER BY JE_HEADER_ID LIMIT 3)
UNION ALL
SELECT * FROM (
    SELECT 'LEGACY',
           doc_ref::VARCHAR,
           entity_cd::VARCHAR,
           txn_dt::VARCHAR,
           amt_incl_tax::VARCHAR,
           NULL::VARCHAR,
           tax_amt::VARCHAR,
           tax_flag::VARCHAR
    FROM RAW_DATA.ERP_LEGACY_AP ORDER BY doc_ref LIMIT 3)
ORDER BY SOURCE_SYSTEM, DOC_REF;

Three systems, three incompatible conventions, one business. DATE_AS_LANDED reads 20250528 in SAP, 2024-05-01 in Oracle and 02/02/2024 in the legacy system. Look at the two amount columns: SAP posts one signed figure (row 2 is negative), Oracle fills either AMOUNT_1 or AMOUNT_2 and leaves the other empty, and the legacy system posts one unsigned figure that already includes the GST.

Snowflake: all three raw shapes queried where they landed. No extract, no staging server, no pre-processing step to maintain.

Why the raw layer stays untidy

The instinct carried over from spreadsheet work is to fix data at the point you find it. Every workshop that starts from a single tidy table skips the part of the job that actually consumes the tax year, and it also teaches a habit that fails an audit: once the landed data has been edited in place, there is no way to separate what the source system sent from what your logic did to it.

The three tax code vocabularies are worth dwelling on separately. SAP's S0 and ZF, Oracle's ZERO_RATED and OUT_OF_SCOPE, and the legacy system's bare Y/N/X are not translations of one another. The legacy flag in particular carries strictly less information than the other two, which is why the conformed ledger in Step 3 derives treatment from the account mapping rather than trusting the source tax code.
